  1. Online Learning Algorithms for Replenishment and Rationing in Two-Class Inventory Systems (Job market paper)

    Xinxiu Wang, Cong Shi, and Xin Geng

    [Abstract]

    We study online learning of replenishment and rationing decisions in a capacitated two-class inventory system with fixed ordering costs. Deterministic high-priority demand must be met in every period, whereas stochastic low-priority demand may be lost; its distribution is unknown, and only sales are observed. Using a fluid approximation, we show that the (s, k, S) policy class contains a first-order asymptotically optimal policy relative to the unrestricted optimum, justifying it as the learning benchmark. Our learning approach combines an observable pseudo cost with an information-dominant policy and reservation-based cycle replay. Each replay round reconstructs one cycle pseudo cost and duration for every candidate from a common sales sample, including censored observations. Rationing can make the objective discontinuous, breaking standard Lipschitz-based discretization. Because discontinuities arise only at a constant number of known inventory levels, our discontinuity-aware grid adds exactly these levels and restores approximation error proportional to the mesh size. We develop GRACE (Grid-based Replay with Adaptive Candidate Elimination), which attains regret O(√(T log T)) relative to this benchmark while revising its policy only logarithmically often; a lower bound shows this rate is worst-case optimal up to a logarithmic factor. Numerical experiments illustrate the gains from adaptive elimination, especially for moderate or high lost-sales penalties.

  2. Direct Selling under Yield Uncertainty and Its Impact on Food Waste

    Xinxiu Wang and Xin Geng

    [Abstract]

    Problem definition: Food waste is a pressing global challenge. Agri-food supply chains in particular often generate losses at the production stage due to yield uncertainty, demand mismatches, and strategic interactions between channel partners. Methodology/results: We develop an analytical model with an upstream farmer and a downstream retailer to study how the farmer’s use of a direct selling channel and the timing of commitment (to direct channel activation) affect equilibrium production, profits, and food waste. Four scenarios are examined, namely, no direct channel, ex ante commitment (before yield realizes), ex post commitment (after yield realizes), and contingent commitment (after observing the retailer’s order); these scenarios represent settings from long-term planning to agile and responsive sales. We also incorporate farmer and retailer environmental awareness, modeled as disutility from food waste, to assess its influence on operational and environmental outcomes. The impact of direct selling on waste hinges on the balance between a sales expansion effect (diverting unsold produce to consumers directly) and an overproduction effect (incentivizing higher supply). Under ex ante commitment, overproduction dominates, raising waste, while under ex post and contingent commitment, the net effect depends on production cost and channel activation cost and thus the resulting food waste may increase due to direct selling. In addition, commitment timing matters. Shifting from ex ante to ex post commitment reduces waste, but further delaying commitment increases it because the retailer orders only the minimum quantity needed to deter direct channel activation, leaving residual output unsold. When incorporating environmental awareness into the farmer’s and the retailer’s objectives, respectively, we find that farmer awareness consistently reduces waste, whereas retailer awareness may increase it. Managerial implications: These findings caution that direct selling and flexibility are not inherently waste-reducing, and that governance of commitment timing, cost structure, and awareness incentives is critical to reducing food waste and realizing environmental gains.

  3. Online Learning for Dynamic Pricing with Endogenous Reviews

    Xinxiu Wang

    [Abstract]

    We study online learning for dynamic pricing when demand depends on price and rating information, and buyers’ ratings may depend on the price paid. The seller observes purchases and ratings but must learn how prices and rating information affect demand and how prices affect ratings. Using a fluid approximation, we show that the optimal fluid value is first-order asymptotically equivalent to the full-information stochastic optimum. Building on this result, we develop state-based fluid reoptimization (SFR), which uses a short pilot phase to decide whether to accumulate ratings at the lowest price before exploring at two prices. This decision balances the revenue cost of waiting against the benefit of diluting exploratory ratings’ effects on future demand. After exploration, SFR estimates market responses and reoptimizes from the evolving rating state. The resulting policy achieves O(√T) regret over T periods relative to the full-information stochastic optimum, with a matching lower bound establishing the optimality of this order. To reduce the frequency of optimization, we also develop a batched version that preserves the optimal regret order with only O(T1/4) fluid re-solves. Numerical experiments show that batching substantially reduces price adjustments with a small increase in relative revenue loss.

  4. Managing Supply Chain Responsibility: The Roles of Channel Structure and Collaborative Effort (Work in progress)

    Xinxiu Wang and Sammi Yu Tang

    [Abstract]

    We study how channel structure affects supply chain responsibility when a buyer shares the cost of a supplier’s effort to prevent violations. Establishing a direct channel increases the supplier’s public exposure to violation-related losses, strengthening her effort incentive but reducing the buyer’s optimal cost sharing. The reduction in buyer support can outweigh the supplier’s stronger incentive, leaving equilibrium violation risk higher than under wholesale-only selling. Public exposure also makes the direct channel less attractive to the supplier. Stronger supplier penalties can therefore induce a switch to wholesale-only, removing the additional effort incentive associated with exposure and causing risk to rise. Our analysis identifies a substitution between supplier self-discipline and buyer support: greater accountability can increase responsibility risk because it changes both the buyer’s willingness to collaborate and the supplier’s channel choice.

  5. Online Learning for Joint Pricing and Inventory Control with Reference Effects (Work in progress)

    Xinxiu Wang

    [Abstract]

    We study joint pricing and inventory control when demand depends on an unknown nonincreasing function of the current price and on an unobserved reference price formed from past prices. In each period the seller chooses a price from a continuous interval and an order quantity, observes demand, and backlogs unmet demand. The baseline demand function, the loss-averse reference-effect parameters, the memory factor, and the noise distribution are unknown. We first show that a constant price and a constant base-stock target attain the full-information dynamic optimum up to an additive constant independent of the horizon. Guided by this benchmark, we propose a policy that tests grid prices in constant-price blocks, eliminates prices with inferior estimated margins, and updates the base-stock target within each block by projected stochastic subgradient steps. The policy requires no knowledge of the reference process or the noise distribution. Its regret relative to the dynamic optimum is O(T2/3 log1/3 T) for a continuous nonincreasing baseline, O(T3/5 log2/5 T) when the baseline margin has a bounded second derivative, and O(√(T log T)) when it also grows quadratically away from its maximizer.
